# Design Pillars

1. **Familiar husbandry, alien biology.** Farming use should make creatures legible as livestock, not Earth-animal reskins. Favor unusual symmetry, limb counts, distributed eyes, inflatable/detachable anatomy, plant-animal symbiosis and strange locomotion.
2. **Shared farm, not private farms.** The map is intentionally small. Useful habitat types and expansion land compete spatially.
3. **Agricola-style opportunity cost.** Limited plots, money, cargo slots, shop stock and upgrades force choices. A run must not allow every upgrade.
4. **Semi-cooperation through competing incentives.** Private goals reward selfish optimization without requiring sabotage.
5. **Emergent chaos over scripted comedy.** Generic systems collide; species-specific jokes are secondary.
6. **Stable biology, variable circumstances.** Species rules remain learnable while strains, population, environment, market and spawned content alter the best strategy.
7. **Colony success comes first.** Personal objectives score only after colony success.
8. **Structurally limit griefing.** Players can care for each other's creatures, but protected breeder/steward stock cannot be permanently erased by another player.

## Guardrails
- Do not build single-player first and network later.
- Do not become a Satisfactory-like factory game.
- Do not make predators turn the game into a shooter.
- Do not author a 50x50 creature relationship matrix.
- Do not start serious production art before the complete ugly match is fun.
- Player UI should communicate symptoms; developers get raw math through debug tools.
